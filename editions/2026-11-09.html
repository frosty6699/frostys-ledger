Corporate Issuers

Capex & project returns

In the newsAnthropic strikes $3.4 billion deal for data centre generators

Capital expenditure is money spent on long-lived assets like plants or data centres. It creates value only if its returns beat the cost of the money used.

Exam angleAccept projects with NPV > 0, which for conventional cash flows means IRR > the cost of capital (WACC).

Alternative Investments

Oil prices & India's economy

In the newsIOC to buy crude, LPG ships as West Asia crisis exposes shipping risks

India imports most of the oil it uses, so pricier crude widens the trade deficit, weakens the rupee and pushes up inflation, all at once.

Exam angleBackwardation (spot above futures) signals tight near-term supply; contango (futures above spot) signals plentiful supply and storage costs.

Financial Statement Analysis

Earnings & margins

Results show whether a company is growing profits, not just sales. Margins show how much of each rupee of revenue survives as profit.

Exam angleGross margin → operating margin (EBIT) → net margin. EBITDA adds back depreciation and amortisation, which are non-cash expenses.

Boards & independent directors

The board is accountable to shareholders for running the company. Independent directors are meant to protect minority shareholders when management and promoters disagree with them.

Exam angleSection 149 of the Companies Act: listed public companies need at least one-third independent directors on their board.

CA Final · Advanced Financial Management

Hedging currency risk

In the newsRupee rises 24 paise to 93.74 against US dollar in early trade

Exporters and importers use forward contracts to lock in today the exchange rate they'll get when the payment actually arrives.

Exam angleForward rate by interest rate parity (direct quote): F = S × (1 + r_home) ÷ (1 + r_foreign). An exporter hedges by selling dollars forward.

Revenue recognition

Revenue is recognised when control of goods or services passes to the customer, not simply when cash arrives or a contract is signed.

About this paper

Frosty’s Ledger prints itself every morning at about 6 AM India time. A small program reads the public RSS feeds of 20 publishers, keeps what’s free to read, puts the same story from different papers together, and links every headline to the original article. Nothing here gets around a paywall: locked stories show only the headline and summary the publisher shares for free.

Headlines, summaries and pictures belong to their publishers. The paper covers the last 30 hours, and links you’ve opened turn grey. Tap any market in the strip at the top for its one-year chart.
