Tariffs & trade

A tariff is a tax on imports. It shields domestic producers but raises prices for consumers and often invites retaliation.

Exam angleFor a small importing country a tariff creates a deadweight loss: consumers lose more than producers and the government gain.

Portfolio Management

Systematic risk

In the newsPassengers and crew foil co-pilot's apparent attempt to crash FlyDubai flight to Israel

In a broad sell-off most stocks fall together: a market-wide shock is a risk you can't diversify away by owning more shares.

Exam angleOnly systematic risk (beta) earns a premium in CAPM: E(R) = Rf + β × [E(Rm) − Rf].

Fixed Income

The yield curve

In the newsUS benchmark yield at its highest since 2002 amid rising oil prices

The yield curve plots yields across maturities. When long-dated yields rise faster than short ones the curve steepens, often a sign of higher expected inflation or heavy government borrowing.

Exam angleKnow the three theories: pure expectations, liquidity preference and market segmentation. An inverted curve (short above long) has often come before recessions.

Revenue is recognised when control of goods or services passes to the customer, not simply when cash arrives or a contract is signed.

Capital budgeting

A new plant or data centre is only worth building if the cash it throws off, discounted at the cost of capital, beats what it costs.

Exam angleAccept if NPV > 0. Payback period is simple but ignores the time value of money and cash flows after payback.

Hedging currency risk

Exporters and importers use forward contracts to lock in today the exchange rate they'll get when the payment actually arrives.

Exam angleForward rate by interest rate parity (direct quote): F = S × (1 + r_home) ÷ (1 + r_foreign). An exporter hedges by selling dollars forward.
