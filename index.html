CFA Level I

Economics

Fiscal deficits & crowding out

In the newsWorld Bank raises India growth forecast to 7.1% for FY27 on strong demand

The fiscal deficit is how much more the government spends than it earns. Financing it means issuing bonds, which competes with companies for the same pool of savings.

Exam angleCrowding out: heavy government borrowing pushes up interest rates and can squeeze private investment.

Corporate Issuers

Mergers & synergies

In the newsUnion Cabinet approves ₹10,000 crore commitment to SME Growth Fund

In an acquisition, the buyer is betting the combined business is worth more than what it pays, through cost savings or new growth: the synergies.

Exam angleSynergy = V(combined) − [V(acquirer) + V(target)]. Pay a premium bigger than the synergies and the value goes to the target's shareholders.

Equity Investments

IPOs & the grey market

In the newsJio Platforms is said to seek about $114 billion valuation in IPO

An IPO is a company's first sale of shares to the public. The grey-market premium is an unofficial pre-listing price signal, not a promise of listing gains.

Exam anglePrimary market: the company raises new capital. Secondary market: investors trade existing shares and the company receives nothing.

CA Final · Advanced Financial Management

Hedging currency risk

In the newsRupee slips 4 paise to 96.39 against US dollar in early trade

Exporters and importers use forward contracts to lock in today the exchange rate they'll get when the payment actually arrives.

Exam angleForward rate by interest rate parity (direct quote): F = S × (1 + r_home) ÷ (1 + r_foreign). An exporter hedges by selling dollars forward.

CA Final · Financial Reporting

Share issue costs in an IPO

In the newsMcDonald's supplier HyFun plans ₹2,000 crore IPO by 2028 as demand rises

An IPO's costs — bankers, lawyers, listing fees — don't all hit the profit and loss account.

Exam angleInd AS 32: costs of issuing new shares are deducted from equity; costs of listing existing shares are expensed. Shared costs are split between the two.

GST & input tax credit

GST is collected at every stage of the supply chain, but each business claims credit for the GST it paid on its inputs, so tax falls only on the value it adds.
