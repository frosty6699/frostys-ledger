CFA Level I

Corporate Issuers

Capex & project returns

In the newsCabinet approves ₹10,000 crore scheme to boost container manufacturing

Capital expenditure is money spent on long-lived assets like plants or data centres. It creates value only if its returns beat the cost of the money used.

Exam angleAccept projects with NPV > 0, which for conventional cash flows means IRR > the cost of capital (WACC).

Financial Statement Analysis

Earnings & margins

In the newsHyundai Motor India Q2 FY27 net profit declines by 24% y-o-y to ₹1,196.1 crore

Results show whether a company is growing profits, not just sales. Margins show how much of each rupee of revenue survives as profit.

Exam angleGross margin → operating margin (EBIT) → net margin. EBITDA adds back depreciation and amortisation, which are non-cash expenses.

Economics

GDP & the business cycle

In the newsFactory output growth slows to 6.7% in September

GDP measures everything an economy produces. Upgrades and downgrades to growth forecasts feed straight into expectations for company earnings.

Exam angleGDP = C + I + G + (X − M). A PMI above 50 signals expansion and works as a leading indicator.

CA Final · Financial Reporting

Share issue costs in an IPO

In the newsJio Platforms IPO surge ends Tata's reign as India's most valuable group

An IPO's costs — bankers, lawyers, listing fees — don't all hit the profit and loss account.

Exam angleInd AS 32: costs of issuing new shares are deducted from equity; costs of listing existing shares are expensed. Shared costs are split between the two.

CA Inter · Financial Management

Capital budgeting

In the newsAdani Enterprises Q2 profit plunges 54% to ₹1,492 crore despite revenue growth

A new plant or data centre is only worth building if the cash it throws off, discounted at the cost of capital, beats what it costs.

Exam angleAccept if NPV > 0. Payback period is simple but ignores the time value of money and cash flows after payback.

CA Inter · Cost & Management Accounting

Contribution & break-even

In the newsMeesho Q2 net loss narrows 68% to ₹132 crore as revenue rises 34%

When input costs rise faster than prices, each unit sold contributes less towards fixed costs, so profit falls faster than sales.

Exam angleContribution = sales − variable costs. Break-even units = fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit; P/V ratio = contribution ÷ sales.
