The CFA & CA Lens

CFA Level I

Financial Statement Analysis

Earnings & margins

In the newsNoel Tata to retire as Trent chairman on Nov 11; Bahram Vakil to take over

Results show whether a company is growing profits, not just sales. Margins show how much of each rupee of revenue survives as profit.

Exam angleGross margin → operating margin (EBIT) → net margin. EBITDA adds back depreciation and amortisation, which are non-cash expenses.

Corporate Issuers

Mergers & synergies

In the newsDoorDash to acquire Instacart for $17 billion

In an acquisition, the buyer is betting the combined business is worth more than what it pays, through cost savings or new growth: the synergies.

Exam angleSynergy = V(combined) − [V(acquirer) + V(target)]. Pay a premium bigger than the synergies and the value goes to the target's shareholders.

Economics

Tariffs & trade

In the newsGoyal says India-US trade deal will show farmers’ interests are protected

A tariff is a tax on imports. It shields domestic producers but raises prices for consumers and often invites retaliation.

Exam angleFor a small importing country a tariff creates a deadweight loss: consumers lose more than producers and the government gain.

CA · ICAI

CA Final · Advanced Auditing & Ethics

Frauds & the auditor's duty

In the newsSC asks RBI to make banks compensate victims of digital arrest, cyber fraud

Auditors don't guarantee that no fraud exists, but they must plan the audit so that material misstatement from fraud has a reasonable chance of being found.

Exam angleSA 240: reasonable, not absolute, assurance. Under Section 143(12) of the Companies Act, the auditor must report suspected fraud above ₹1 crore to the Central Government.

CA Inter · Corporate & Other Laws

Dividends & buybacks under the Act

In the newsAsian Paints Q2 Results: Net profit rises 7% YoY to Rs 1,068 crore; firm announces Rs 5.75/share dividend

Company law limits how much cash a company can hand back, to protect its creditors.

Exam angleDividends only out of profits (Section 123). Buybacks capped at 25% of paid-up capital and free reserves, with debt no more than twice capital and free reserves afterwards (Section 68).

CA Final · Financial Reporting

Related-party transactions

In the newsAmbuja Cements Q2 net profit plunges 90 pc as margins come under pressure

Deals between a company and its promoters, directors or group firms can move value out of minority shareholders' hands, so the law makes them disclose and seek approval.

Exam angleInd AS 24 requires the relationships and transactions to be disclosed. Section 188 of the Companies Act needs board approval, and shareholders' approval above the thresholds.
